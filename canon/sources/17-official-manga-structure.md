HP: REVENGE — Official Manga Structure
Season 1: The Broken Crown · Production Blueprint v1
We're structuring this as a serialized, right-to-left, black-and-white manga with cinematic pacing, substantial character development, and longer chapters for major confrontations.
This is the production structure only: chapter counts, page allocation, panel rules, pacing, and publication organization. No actual manga pages yet.

Each of the 24 planned anime-style episodes maps to two manga chapters. Page allocations vary by story needs; 1,056 is the fixed initial production budget.
2. Chapter and volume organization
￼
The 22-page average is a planning target, not a requirement that every chapter must have identical pacing.
3. Chapter length rules
￼
Important: The final 48-chapter, 1,056-page budget will be balanced during detailed chapter allocation. Longer chapters must be offset by shorter chapters elsewhere.
4. Page composition system




Standard
A. Standard narrative page
4–6 panels. Used for conversations, school life, relationship development, and movement between locations.
Clear right-to-left reading order, natural dialogue placement, and stable character geography.




Action
B. Action page
2–5 panels. Used for confrontations and physical movement.
Wider panels establish positioning. Narrower panels emphasize reactions and timing. The reader must always understand who is where.


Splash
C. Full-page splash
One large illustration. Reserved for major entrances, important emotional revelations, iconic compositions, and climactic moments.
No more than approximately one per standard chapter unless justified.



Double spread
D. Double-page spread
Two facing pages form one composition. Reserved for exceptional moments.
Important faces, lettering, and critical details must avoid the center binding gutter.
5. Season pacing allocation
Page budget by narrative function
￼

This is an editorial allocation rather than a requirement to separate those elements. A fight can simultaneously develop a relationship, and an ordinary school conversation can advance the mystery.
6. Fight page budgets
￼
The final duel should span approximately three chapters, with the emotional aftermath included in the final chapter.
Its extra length must come from changing momentum, meaningful decisions, character memories, and emotional development—not repeating the same attacks.
7. Standard chapter architecture
22-page standard chapter





￼
Not every chapter needs a cliffhanger. Quiet endings and moments of closure are important to avoid making the manga feel artificially dramatic.
8. Reading and lettering rules
Reading direction: Japanese-style right to left, top to bottom.  
Artwork: Primarily black and white with screentones, selective solid blacks, and controlled line weight.  
Dialogue: Natural, concise, character-specific. In scripts, preserve the established "Dialogue" - said Characterattribution format; final manga pages use speech balloons.  
Speech balloons: Reading order must remain unambiguous, with text placed before artwork is finalized.  
Page turns: Major reveals should land after a page turn when possible.  
Flashbacks: Consistent visual treatment and clearly identifiable time transitions.  
Locations: Establishing panels whenever the scene changes to a new or potentially confusing setting.  
Continuity: Character outfits, injuries, gang affiliations, locations, and membership changes tracked by chapter.  
9. Production stages
Chapter script — Dialogue, scene order, character actions, and intended emotional beats.  
Page breakdown — Assign each beat to specific pages, including reveals and page turns.  
Thumbnail storyboard — Rough panel layouts and reading flow.  
Character and background pass — Consistent designs, poses, locations, and perspective.  
Inking and tones — Finished black-and-white artwork with visual hierarchy.  
Lettering — Dialogue balloons, captions, sound effects, and legibility review.  
Continuity and quality check — Verify story logic, panel order, visual consistency, and text accuracy.  
Final export — Numbered page images, chapter packages, and collected-volume layouts.  
10. Final production specification
￼
Structure established: 48 chapters across six volumes, with a deliberately expanded final duel and a consistent page-production system.
The next planning layer is the exact Chapter 1–48 allocation: chapter titles, page counts, opening and closing beats, character appearances, and which story events occur on which pages.
