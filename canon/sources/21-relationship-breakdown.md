Relationship breakdown


BROTHER. This is where HP: Revenge goes from six gangs with cool character designs to an actual interconnected universe.
And I think there's one decision that will make or break the entire story:
Jack's betrayal cannot be the beginning of the conflict. It has to be the consequence of conflicts that have been developing since before Episode 1.
The best part of Tokyo Revengers isn't simply that people betray each other. It's that friendships, loyalties, misunderstandings, and decisions accumulate until characters end up fighting people they once would have protected.
I want that same emotional complexity here, without copying its plot.
HP: REVENGE — Relationship Bible
STORY ARCHITECTURE · SIX GANGS · 60 ROSTER POSITIONS
1. The foundation: These gangs were once connected
Before the six gangs became rivals, their founding members belonged to overlapping social circles.
Some grew up together. Some went to the same middle school. Some were teammates. Others became friends through mutual friends.
The crucial thing is that the six gangs aren't six groups of strangers.
Proposed network of rivalries, alliances and personal history. Lines represent meaningful connections, not necessarily friendships.
The central relationships should be Jackson–Jack, Bryson–Isaac, Jackson–Isaac, Jack–Dev, and Jackson–Bryson.
Everything else gradually grows out of those five connections.
2. The four relationships that carry the story
Jackson Anderson and Jack Davenport
The central tragedy
Jackson and Jack are genuinely best friends at the beginning.
Jackson is the strategist, the person who sees the bigger picture. Jack is the person who will stand beside him when everyone else backs down.
Their friendship is not based on convenience. They've been through enough together that neither can imagine the other becoming an enemy.
But they have one fundamental difference:
Jackson believes loyalty means trusting each other. Jack believes loyalty means never abandoning each other.
Those sound identical until Jackson makes a decision Jack considers abandonment.
Their progression:
Beginning: Completely inseparable. Jack is HP's second-in-command, and nobody questions his position.  
Early tension: Jackson increasingly makes difficult leadership decisions without explaining everything to Jack.  
First fracture: Jackson refuses to intervene in a conflict because doing so would endanger the entire gang. Jack sees someone who needs help and intervenes anyway.  
Growing resentment: Jack begins to believe Jackson cares more about protecting HP's reputation than protecting individual friends.  
Dev's influence: Dev doesn't create Jack's doubts. He gives Jack a place where those doubts are understood.  
The betrayal: Jack eventually leads Tsukabi, taking people who trust him. He isn't trying to destroy Jackson; he believes Jackson has lost sight of what HP was supposed to represent.  
Aftermath: Neither can bring himself to treat the other like an ordinary enemy.  
The important detail is that Jack should never suddenly become cruel or evil.
He should still protect Jackson in certain circumstances, even after betraying him. And Jackson should hesitate when an opportunity arises to completely eliminate Tsukabi as a rival.
Their conflict is painful precisely because both remember who the other used to be.
Bryson French and Isaac Diaz
The unbreakable partnership
This is the relationship I would make the emotional opposite of Jackson and Jack.
Bryson and Isaac do not betray each other.
Not every friendship needs to collapse for the story to be interesting.
Bryson is Branch's leader. Isaac is his second-in-command. Bryson is the steady, commanding presence; Isaac is unpredictable, impulsive, and exceptionally dangerous when emotionally invested.
Bryson understands Isaac better than almost anyone.
Isaac can be joking around one moment and completely serious the next. Other people interpret that as immaturity. Bryson knows when Isaac is genuinely upset.
Their dynamic:
Bryson makes the final decisions, but Isaac is one of the few people allowed to challenge him openly.  
Isaac can disagree with Bryson without threatening their friendship.  
Bryson doesn't try to change Isaac's personality. He gives Isaac direction.  
Isaac trusts Bryson's judgment even when he doesn't fully understand it.  
When Bryson makes a mistake, Isaac tells him. He doesn't blindly defend every decision.  
Their biggest storyline shouldn't be a betrayal. It should be the pressure of remaining loyal when everyone expects them to turn against each other.
At some point, Isaac should have an opportunity to gain power by abandoning Bryson.
He refuses.
Not because he lacks ambition, but because he values their friendship more than a title.
That moment would hit much harder in a universe where betrayals are common.
Jackson Anderson and Isaac Diaz
Friendship across enemy lines
This relationship has enormous potential.
Jackson and Isaac have an established friendship that predates the current gang conflict.
Even though Isaac is Branch's second-in-command, he doesn't suddenly forget that Jackson is his friend.
At first, their relationship is almost comedic.
They can be talking normally, making jokes, and insulting each other while everyone around them is tense because their gangs are rivals.
But as HP and Branch become increasingly hostile, maintaining that friendship becomes difficult.
The defining moment: Jackson asks Isaac for information that could protect HP but potentially expose Branch.
Isaac refuses.
Jackson understands intellectually, but it still hurts.
Later, Isaac asks Jackson for a favor that could compromise HP. Jackson also refuses.
Neither is necessarily wrong.
Eventually they learn that friendship doesn't entitle either of them to the other's gang secrets.
Their relationship survives because they stop asking each other to choose.
Jackson Anderson and Bryson French
Rival leaders · Mutual respect
Jackson and Bryson should be rivals without being cartoonishly hostile.
Both are natural leaders, but they approach leadership differently.
Jackson leads through intelligence, ambition, charisma, and calculated decisions.
Bryson leads through stability, trust, and his ability to make people feel protected.
Jackson thinks Bryson is sometimes too cautious.
Bryson thinks Jackson sometimes treats people like pieces on a chessboard.
Their rivalry becomes interesting because each possesses something the other lacks.
At first, they compete for influence and territory.
Eventually, an external threat forces them into temporary cooperation.
They discover they're remarkably effective together.
And that makes their eventual disagreement even more dangerous.
Neither wants to destroy the other, but neither is willing to surrender his principles.

3. The other relationships that make the universe feel alive
The supporting cast cannot exist solely to fill out gang rosters. Every gang needs internal friendships, disagreements, and characters who have lives outside their leader.

Luke Hornbach → Jackson and Jack
Luke is the social glue of HP. He notices Jackson and Jack becoming distant before either admits anything is wrong. He initially tries to repair the friendship instead of choosing a side. When the split finally happens, Luke's disappointment should be more devastating than his anger.


Jason Schmidt → Jack
Jason is younger, constantly trying to prove himself, and idolizes Jack's confidence. He copies Jack's mannerisms and wants his approval. When Jack leaves HP, Jason follows him to Tsukabi, believing that's what loyalty means. His arc is learning to make decisions independently.


Chris Harding and Ethan Caldwell → Jackson
Chris understands Jackson's calculations better than most. Ethan understands their consequences. Chris might argue that Jackson's decisions are strategically correct, while Ethan questions whether they are morally justified. Their disagreements make HP's leadership feel real.


Leo and Niko Kottos → Each other
They are practically inseparable. Leo is loud, competitive, and confrontational; Niko is sarcastic and slightly more observant. They argue constantly but become serious when the other is threatened. Their relationship provides comedy without making them useless in serious moments.


Ren and Robert → Kurogetsu
Ren is physically imposing and commands attention effortlessly. Robert is comparatively weak but extremely intelligent. Ren takes Robert seriously when stronger members dismiss him. Robert is one of the only people willing to tell Ren when he's making a terrible decision.


Kai and Haru Kage → Shiranui
Kai is a gifted fighter, but Haru understands people and notices details. Kai trusts Haru to challenge his assumptions. Their relationship is quieter than Bryson and Isaac's, based more on mutual respect than emotional openness.


Marcus Vale and Adrian Mercer → Kaien
Marcus is brilliant but secretive. Adrian is disciplined and believes leadership requires accountability. Adrian begins as Marcus's strongest supporter but gradually questions how much Marcus is withholding. Their conflict is about trust, not jealousy.
4. How Jack's betrayal actually happens
This is the storyline I'd build the first major saga around.
And there's an important continuity solution: HP and Tsukabi's posters represent different points in the story. Jack, Jason, and Max aren't inexplicably full-time members of two rival gangs simultaneously. Tsukabi's final lineup forms after the split.
THE FALL OF HP'S BROTHERHOOD
The promise Jackson and Jack establish HP around protecting their friends. Jackson becomes leader because he's the natural organizer. Jack accepts second-in-command because he trusts Jackson, not because he considers himself inferior.  
The impossible decision A dispute involving younger members escalates. Jackson discovers that immediately retaliating would endanger several HP members and potentially cause school expulsions. He chooses negotiation. Jack sees the delay as abandoning someone who trusted them.  
The first disobedience Jack intervenes without Jackson's permission. He helps the person he believes needs protecting, but his actions undermine Jackson's negotiations. Both believe they acted responsibly.  
The punishment Jackson temporarily removes Jack's authority over gang operations. This is the critical mistake: Jackson intends to restore discipline, but Jack experiences it as public humiliation by his closest friend.  
Dev's invitation Dev, who already has his own small circle, offers Jack a place to talk without being judged. Dev doesn't initially demand anything. He simply asks whether Jackson still sees Jack as an equal.  
The secret meeting Jack begins meeting with Dev, Leo, Niko, and others. Jackson hears rumors but refuses to believe Jack would organize anything behind his back.  
The departure Jack tells Jackson he is leaving HP. Jason follows Jack. Max eventually joins Tsukabi as well. Other HP members consider leaving but remain. The departure should be public enough to change the balance of power, but the most important conversation happens privately between Jackson and Jack.  
The betrayal that changes everything Much later, Jack uses knowledge of HP's internal plans to outmaneuver Jackson during a major gang confrontation. This is when Jackson finally calls it betrayal. Leaving HP was painful. Using their shared history against him is what breaks his trust.  
Jack shouldn't immediately regret leaving. For a while, Tsukabi should actually thrive under his leadership.
That makes the conflict harder: Jackson cannot dismiss Jack's new gang as a failure, and Jack cannot dismiss Jackson as incompetent.
5. The six-gang political structure
Each gang should have its own interests. They shouldn't all spend every episode obsessing over Jackson.
￼
The conflicts should come from competing goals rather than everyone simply wanting to fight.
For example, Ren might want control of a meeting place that Branch considers neutral ground. Bryson refuses. Jackson offers mediation, but Kai opposes the proposed arrangement because it threatens Shiranui's access.
Suddenly four gangs are involved, each for a believable reason.
Marcus doesn't need to manufacture every conflict. He can simply recognize opportunities within disputes that already exist.
6. Season 1 — Major story beats
Instead of fixing episode numbers immediately, I'd organize the first season into six narrative phases. These can expand or contract depending on pacing.




PHASE I · THE GOLDEN DAYS
The gang that couldn't be broken
Introduce Jackson, Jack, Luke, Jason, and the rest of HP through ordinary school life, friendships, arguments, and small rivalries. Bryson and Isaac appear early, not as villains but as people with their own friendships.
End the phase with a minor incident that exposes Jackson and Jack's different ideas about leadership.





PHASE II · SIX FLAGS
The city begins choosing sides
Introduce Ren, Kai, and Marcus through separate storylines. Establish territories, friendships, and rivalries.
Jack begins questioning Jackson's decisions. Meanwhile, Isaac gets caught between his friendship with Jackson and his responsibilities to Bryson.





PHASE III · THE CRACK
A friendship begins to fracture
Jack openly disobeys Jackson. Luke tries to mediate. Dev becomes increasingly important to Jack.
Kurogetsu and Branch clash over a disputed meeting place. Jackson's attempt to prevent escalation creates resentment inside HP.





PHASE IV · TSUKABI RISES
The brother who walked away
Jack leaves. Dev becomes his second-in-command. Tsukabi emerges as an independent gang.
Jackson refuses to acknowledge how deeply the split affected him. Bryson notices. Isaac attempts to keep the situation from spiraling.





PHASE V · THE FALSE PEACE
Enemies share a table
Marcus proposes a meeting between the six leaders after several disputes threaten everyone's stability.
The meeting initially succeeds. But its agreements disproportionately benefit Kaien, and Haru begins investigating why.





PHASE VI · BROKEN PROMISES
The first true betrayal
Tsukabi and HP clash over an agreement Jack believes Jackson violated.
Jack reveals that he knows HP's private contingency plans. Jackson realizes his former best friend has used their history against him.
The season ends without a definitive victor. HP survives, Tsukabi gains recognition, and the six-gang balance is permanently altered.
7. The deeper storylines for later seasons
I wouldn't reveal everything in Season 1. We need mysteries and developments that can sustain multiple seasons.
The original Kaien connection
Kaien should be the oldest surviving organization in the current gang system, with connections to former students and older members. Marcus inherited some of those relationships, but not all of their loyalties. His real objective is to prevent a past conflict from repeating itself, even if that means manipulating the other gangs.

Isaac's impossible position
A later conflict between HP and Branch becomes personal when someone Isaac cares about is caught in the middle. Bryson tells Isaac he doesn't have to participate. Isaac chooses to remain by Bryson's side while privately trying to prevent Jackson from being harmed. This tests his loyalty without forcing a cheap betrayal.

Ren's leadership crisis
Ren's reputation attracts ambitious members who believe strength is all that matters. Robert warns him that fear is not the same as loyalty. Ren must decide whether Kurogetsu will remain a brotherhood or become an organization held together through intimidation.

Kai's hidden history
Kai once trusted someone who broke an important promise, explaining his strict attitude toward loyalty. He doesn't need a supernatural secret or some enormous conspiracy. A believable personal history can explain why he acts so differently from Jackson and Bryson.

The second generation
Jason, Leo, Niko, Milo, and other younger or less experienced members gradually become more influential. The leaders eventually realize that their personal conflicts are shaping how the younger members understand friendship, power, and loyalty.
8. The relationship rules I'd make permanent canon
These are the principles that keep the universe consistent.
Bryson and Isaac stay loyal to each other. They can argue, fail, and disagree, but their friendship is one of the story's constants.  
Jackson and Jack genuinely love each other as brothers before the split. Their eventual hostility must never erase that history.  
Jack's betrayal is gradual. No sudden personality change, mind control, or ridiculous secret villain explanation.  
Every gang has internal politics. Members have their own opinions and do not automatically agree with their leader.  
Marcus is intelligent, not omniscient. He makes mistakes and cannot secretly control every event.  
Strength does not guarantee leadership. Robert, Dev, Chris, and other intelligent characters can meaningfully influence stronger fighters.  
Relationships have consequences. A betrayal should affect how people behave many episodes later, not disappear after one apology.  
There are no disposable gangs. Every organization gets moments of success, weakness, friendship, and meaningful development.  

9. The biggest long-term payoff
Here's the scene I would build toward, potentially multiple seasons into the story.
Jackson and Jack are forced into a temporary alliance against a common threat. They haven't trusted each other in a long time.
During a tense confrontation, Jack instinctively steps forward to protect Jackson, just like he used to.
Jackson notices.
Neither says anything.
Later, when they're alone:
"You still do that." - said Jackson
"Do what?" - said Jack
"Stand in front of me when things get bad." - said Jackson
"Old habit." - said Jack
"Thought you got rid of those." - said Jackson
"Not all of them." - said Jack
And that's it. No immediate reconciliation. No dramatic speech. They go their separate ways.
That kind of scene only works if we've spent dozens of episodes establishing who these two were, what they lost, and why they can't simply return to the past.
The core of HP: Revenge should ultimately be this: six gangs fighting over power and loyalty, while the most important battles are between people who once trusted each other completely.
Jackson and Jack are the tragedy. Bryson and Isaac are the counterexample. Ren and Kai represent competing ideas of strength. Marcus represents the danger of intelligence without transparency.
That's the foundation I'd use for the entire series.
